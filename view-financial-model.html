Financial Model

Twelve tabs. Assumptions hold every input; every other tab derives. Nothing is typed twice.

The tabs, and the job of each

  • Index — names every tab and what it answers.
  • Assumptions — every input in one place, each labelled measured, stated position, or blind.
  • Pilot economics — one engagement: its hours, its cost, its effective rate.
  • Revenue build — twenty-four months, derived rather than typed.
  • Cost build — fixed base, the next hire, delivery cost.
  • Cash runway — opening cash, monthly net, closing balance, and the state of each month.
  • Valuation band — a range with its basis stated, never a single point.
  • Deal structure — headline against certain-at-close, contingent shown apart.
  • Milestones — four gates, each with what must be true first.
  • Blind register — every unlit figure, and the record that would light it.
  • Founders read — the council's verdict on the numbers.
  • About — who we are, and the client fields still blind.

The rules the model obeys

  • A band is never reported as a point. A band whose low equals its high is refused.
  • An earnings multiple is never applied to a loss. Where it does not apply, the model says so rather than printing a number.
  • Contingent consideration is shown separately, never discounted at an invented probability.
  • Where two sources disagree, both are named and the gap is shown rather than resolved by preference.

The pilot, shown honestly

Measured — pilot fee $25,000, fixed and agreed before work starts.
Stated position — 120 hours across four roles at a $350 target rate, giving an effective rate near $208 and a deliberate $17,000 gap against target. The pilot is priced to prove the method, not to make the year.
Blind — no pilot has closed, so the 120 hours remains a position. One completed pilot lights it.
