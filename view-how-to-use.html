How to use these documents

Five documents do five different jobs. Used in the wrong order they repeat each other; used in this order each one feeds the next and nothing is typed twice.

The five documents

1 · Executive Deck — nineteen slides

  • Opens the room. Problem, method, pilot and ask in under twenty minutes.
  • Never hand it over as a read-alone: the speaker notes carry half the argument.

2 · Business Plan, working document — ten parts

  • Does the work. Fill-in fields, check-offs, and a blind register that refuses to be guessed.
  • Run it as a session, never as homework.

3 · Business Plan, worked example

  • Shows what finished looks like, so nobody guesses at the standard.
  • Borrow the standard of answer, never the answers themselves.

4 · Financial Model — twelve tabs

  • Holds the arithmetic. Assumptions in one place, every later tab derived.
  • Open it only once Parts 2 to 6 of the plan are filled.

5 · This guide

  • Read first, kept beside the set.

The order of work

Order matters more than pace. A session completing Parts 1 and 2 honestly beats one filling all ten with guesses.

Step 1 · Present the deck

Stop at the pilot slide. The deck exists to reach one question: which decision are you least comfortable making on the evidence you hold today? That answer sets everything after it.

Step 2 · Open the plan at Part 1

Record the decision, its owner, its date and the success measure. Twenty minutes, and the only part that cannot be skipped.

Step 3 · Work Parts 2 to 6

Answer from records with the worked example beside you. Where no record answers, mark it blind and move on.

Step 4 · Fill the model

Enter the amber inputs; every other tab derives. Read three figures back into Part 7: the lowest cash month, the month the next hire must start, and the revenue covering it.

Step 5 · Close Parts 8 to 10

Ninety days, the blind register, and sign-off. Part 9 collects every blind field with an owner and a review date.

Who leads what

  • Decision owner — answers Part 1, signs Part 10, and alone may change the success measure.
  • Numbers owner — fills the workbook and owns the Part 7 figures.
  • Operating owner — answers Parts 4 and 8, knowing where work actually stops.
  • DashRite — runs the session, holds the labels honest, refuses to fill a blind field.
The rule that protects the work. Nobody marks their own homework. Whoever fills a section is never the person who signs it off.

Common mistakes, and the cure

Filling blinds to look finished

Destroys the only advantage the method has. Read Part 9 aloud at the end and be proud of its length.

Typing a figure into two documents

It will drift, and the drift surfaces in front of a client. Workbook holds arithmetic, plan quotes it, deck quotes the plan.

Sending the plan as homework

It returns half-filled with guesses, and the guesses get treated as records. Book the session instead.

Letting the success measure move

A measure agreed afterwards is a negotiation. Part 1 is signed before work starts.
