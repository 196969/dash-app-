Business Plan — worked example

The same ten parts, completed. It exists to show the standard of answer, never to lend you its facts.

Stated position — every figure on this page belongs to another engagement. Borrow the standard, never the answer.

What a finished Part 1 looks like

The decision is named in one sentence, its owner is a person rather than a committee, the date is a date rather than a quarter, and the success measure carries a number that can be tested afterwards.

  • Decision: whether to acquire, at what price, on what evidence.
  • Owner: the principal who signs, named.
  • Measure: one testable sentence, agreed in writing at kickoff.
  • Failure condition: written beside it, because a measure without one is not a measure.

What a finished Part 2 looks like

Each figure traces to a system. Revenue comes from the ledger rather than the pipeline, concentration is calculated rather than estimated, and the close and reconciliation dates are real dates.

Measured — the figures in this section each name the system they came from, so any one of them can be challenged at source.

What a finished blind looks like

Blind — market size by firm count. What would light it: a sourced count of firms in the geography, average account size, spend per engagement. Owner named, review date set.

Notice what the finished version does not do. It does not estimate. It does not round toward the answer the author wanted. It names the record that would settle it and moves on, which is the whole reason a reader trusts the sections that are filled.

What a finished Part 6 looks like

Every risk row carries three things and never fewer: the exposure today, the mitigation with a date, and an owner who is a person. The two largest risks appear first rather than the two easiest.

What a finished Part 9 looks like

The blind register is long, and the author is comfortable with that. Each entry names the figure, where it appears, and the record that would light it. A short register in a young business is a warning sign rather than a credential.
